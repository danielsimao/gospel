# "Good Enough" — shelved

**Status:** built 2026-07-27, removed the same day at the owner's call. Nothing
of it ships. This file exists so the idea and the reasoning survive; the code is
recoverable from `dac48b8`, and the full design record from that commit's
version of `2026-07-27-good-enough-build.md`.

## The idea

A page at `/good-enough` where the reader taps a button to fill a bar toward a
line marked **Required**. Fill it and you're in. The bar rises in equal steps
for eight taps and then stops dead, far below the line. The button is never
taken away — keep pressing and a hard lip renders across the fill and the copy
narrates that nothing is moving. Then Romans 3:23 and a link to `/test`.

**Origin:** owner's — *"if you click this button, you will go to heaven, but the
button is impossible to click, and we end up giving the gospel."*

## What was learned, and is worth keeping whatever gets built next

**Never a near miss.** The most replicated finding in gambling research: near
misses *raise* the motivation to continue and breed an illusion of control. Any
version of this idea that lets the reader get close teaches *try harder*, which
is the belief the page exists to remove. It is also why an asymptote — tempting,
because "approaches but never reaches" is literally the doctrine — is exactly
wrong: it is a near-miss generator by construction.

**The standard may be hidden; the rules never may.** Secret decay, a mis-tap
penalty, or a button that dodges the cursor make it a carnival game, and the
response to a carnival game is *you tricked me*, not repentance. The Good Person
Test convicts precisely because its arithmetic is fair and visible.

**No timer.** A clock makes the failure *unstable* ("I ran out of time") and
*specific* ("my tapping speed") — the two attributions that send someone back
for another go. It also imports the wrong illustration: a countdown argues
urgency, Comfort's parachute; this argues impossibility, Romans 3:23.

**Let the reader stop, not the page.** Removing the button says the machine quit
on you. Leaving it live lets them prove for themselves that more effort changes
nothing.

**A variable outcome plus a replay is a slot machine** unless the variance is
visibly irrelevant — no roll anywhere near the line.

**Inability is not guilt.** The strongest reason the CTA had to go to `/test`
rather than to grace: a reader holding only "it can't be earned" concludes
either *nobody gets in* or *God lets everyone in*, and neither is the gospel.
The Law has to get specific. Any general illustration manufactures the need for
the test rather than replacing it.

**What did not work:** drawing other people's bars to make the failure global. A
spread is a distribution, and a distribution implies a tail — the eye hunts for
the tallest and infers somebody might reach the line.

## Why it was dropped

Not because the mechanic failed — it worked, and was verified. It was a second
front door competing for attention on a site whose funnel already points
everywhere at `/test`, built for sharing that is not happening yet. Revisit if
and when there is traffic to share it with.
