# The method

This app is a Living Waters / Way of the Master presentation. That is not a
style choice layered on top of a product — it is the product, and the design
serves it. Every screen exists because the method has a step there.

**Fact-check copy and flow changes against this document.** It exists because
the method was being reconstructed from memory on each pass, and things that are
load-bearing were quietly removed by people (including me) who did not know they
were load-bearing.

`.claude/skills/copy-review` is the checklist for reviewing a diff. This is the
reference the checklist is drawn from — the positive statement of what the app
is doing and why.

---

## The spine

> **Law to the proud, grace to the humble.**

The Law is not a preamble to the good news. It is the instrument that makes the
good news legible: someone who does not know they are guilty has no use for a
pardon. Romans 3:20 — *by the law is the knowledge of sin*. Galatians 3:24 — the
law as the tutor that brings us to Christ.

Two colours carry it through the whole app:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Red** | judgment, the Law, the verdict |
| **Gold** | grace, mercy, the way out |

Gold does not appear during the Law. It arrives once, and its arrival is the
event. Do not spend it early.

## The order, and why each step is where it is

### 1 · The question, before the Law

*"Are you a good person?"* — asked before anything is proved, so the reader's own
answer becomes testimony that can be quoted back to them.

It is **testimony, never a measurement.** Nothing in the scoring path may read
it. A reader who answers "no" still takes all six questions: "grace to the
humble" means humbled *by the Law*, and "I'm not perfect" is usually social
modesty rather than conviction. Shortening the Law on a self-report is the
shortcut the method exists to prevent.

### 2 · The Law, one commandment at a time

Specific, never general. *Have you ever told a lie?* — not *are you generally
a good person*. The specificity is the instrument; vague copy blunts it.

Each admission is followed by the standard that convicts it (Matthew 5:28 for
lust, and so on). The follow-up presses the evasion rather than accepting it.

### 3 · The verdict

Guilty, by their own admission, on their own answers. James 2:10 — *keep the
whole law and stumble in one point, guilty of all* — is the exact argument the
six questions build, so the app cites the Law rather than asserting guilt in its
own voice.

The confession sentence distinguishes what was **admitted** from what was
**evaded**. Evasions are recorded, not dismissed. Rendering both in one colour
would state the evaded commandments with the same force as the confessed ones,
which is not what the reader said.

### 4 · Grace — the courtroom analogy

Ray Comfort's analogy, in its load-bearing order:

1. **You are guilty and the penalty is real.** A fine you cannot pay; the wage
   of sin is death.
2. **Justice cannot simply waive it.** A good judge who dismisses a proved crime
   is not merciful, he is corrupt. This is why forgiveness needs a mechanism.
3. **Someone pays the fine in full.** The judge can now dismiss the case
   *legally* — not because justice was overlooked but because it was satisfied.
4. **That someone is Jesus Christ** — God in flesh, who lived the life you
   couldn't, died the death you deserved, and rose.
5. **Repent and trust.** Turn from sin, trust in Christ alone.

Step 2 is load-bearing for step 3 and cannot be cut: without the judge and the
courtroom, "someone pays" has no venue and no legal force.

Step 5 is the response, and it is **never** "say this prayer" or "make a
decision". See *No decisionism* below.

### 5 · The decision

A real choice, with its alternatives visible and none of them hidden. The reader
must be free to decline **and it must be visible that they are free**. A screen
that makes declining hard is doing something the method forbids.

### 6 · After the decision — the courtroom stops

Discipleship copy is plain and warm, never themed. Extending the courtroom past
the decision — rehab, probation, going straight, staying clean, a record to
maintain — tells a pardoned reader they are under correctional supervision,
which inverts the sentence the whole app exists to deliver.

**The fine was paid in full. Adoption, not parole.**

## The rules that are not negotiable

### No decisionism

Nothing may promise that a prayer, a tap, or a button saves. Commitment copy
stays conditional and points to Christ, never to the act of deciding.

*"Today is the beginning"* is acceptable — it says something has started.
*"You are saved"* after a tap is not.

### Grace is never offered before the Law has done its work

Comfort on this: grace offered to the proud is a pearl before swine. Copy that
jumps to comfort on a judgment screen is a method error, not a tone preference.

### The indicative before the imperative

*Someone paid your fine* is announced as accomplished fact before the reader is
told to repent and trust. That is Pauline order, and it is why grace can answer
"is there any hope?" immediately rather than three beats later.

The **volume** of that announcement matters, though: stated small it names a
topic; stated large and alone it declares something about *this reader*.
"But God…" (Ephesians 2:4) above it frames the line as the scriptural turn
rather than a personal guarantee.

### Mortality is a stake, not a lever

The death counter is real and it belongs in the flow. It must not be positioned
so that it reads as pressure applied at the moment of choosing — between the
question and the answer is the wrong place for it.

## Committed vocabulary

Full reasoning in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-07-27-vocabulary-map.md`.

| term | status |
|---|---|
| **Test** | never rename — Living Waters' own name (the Good Person Test), and load-bearing in `/test`, the metadata and the site identity |
| **"But God…"** | Ephesians 2:4, the theological hinge — unchanged |
| **"Someone paid your fine"** | confirmed against Comfort's courtroom analogy |
| **"Seven days in John"**, **"Honest answers"**, **"Pass it on"**, **"The Law"** | plain by design; do not re-theme |
| **"The 180"** | **forbidden** — Comfort's 2011 pro-life documentary, a different subject entirely |

**Portuguese** is `tu` throughout where the reader is addressed; third person
where the copy is about Jesus. The word for *fine* in grace is **`dívida`**
(ruled 2026-07-31); `multa` still appears across `learn` and has not been swept,
because several passages there use both words inside one paragraph and that may
be deliberate variation.

## Known gaps, open at the time of writing

**The penalty is no longer described as eternal.** Grace's first beat once read
*"You're guilty. The fine is eternal"*; it was cut on 2026-07-31 because it
restated the verdict. The surviving beat keeps *justice demands death* and *you
can't pay*, so the **inability** survives — but the only remaining
"eternal"/"everlasting" in the flow are on the grace side (*eternal life*, John
3:16). In Comfort's presentation the infinite penalty is *why* the fine cannot
be paid. Unresolved.

**Grace's four beats now carry one Law beat and three of grace**, so the hinge
falls after the first rather than the middle. Deliberate, but it means the Law
side of the courtroom rests almost entirely on the verdict screen.

## How to use this

Before changing copy or flow in `/test`, grace or the decision, ask:

1. Which step of the method is this screen doing?
2. Does the change remove something the next step depends on?
3. Does it move grace earlier, or the Law later?
4. Does it make declining harder, or make a tap look like it saves?
5. Is a committed term being renamed or re-themed?

If the answer to 2–5 is yes for any of them, it is a method change and belongs
to the owner, not to whoever is holding the keyboard.
