Tem volta?

Everyone in Portugal now asks it at the bottle bin. Asked of a person, it means: is there a way back? The machine only accepts what is empty, intact and has the symbol. So, would you be accepted?

The bridge

The ritual carries the question, not the doctrine

The metaphor is a light touch: the hook, the receipt, and one deliberate break at the cross. The Law, the Judge and the debt stay in the method's own words, because a dented bottle is damage and sin is guilt.

Three ways in

What people actually meet first

Why this name

"Tem volta?" over "Volta Jesus"

It's a question

Questions pull people in, and this one is already said every week at the bin. It promises nothing, so no grace is offered before the Law.

It doesn't filter people out

"Volta Jesus" reads as a church banner before anyone is curious. Written "Volta, Jesus", it means "Come back, Jesus!".

It isn't the brand

volta.com.pt is SDR Portugal's official site and Volta® is registered. We use an everyday phrase, our own look and a visible disclaimer.

Guardrails

What keeps it honest

Never pass as the system

No Volta logo, "V" mark, colours or domain anywhere. Every card and the tract carry: "Sem ligação ao sistema Volta de embalagens."

No bait-and-switch

God is named on card 2 and on the tract's back. A page that tricked you can't then ask whether you've ever lied.

Sin is guilt, not a dent

The receipt lists what the reader did, in their own answers. Judgment stays with the Judge and the debt, never with a sensor.

Respect for Catholic Portugal

"O batismo, a missa, as orações" is answered with "São coisas boas. Nenhuma delas é o paraquedas." Confession is "a Deus".

No fake vouchers

The tract says "Não é válido em loja" on the front. It is handed to people, never left on machines or store property.

The method, unchanged

The reader gives the verdict. Grace only after it. Three identical exits. No "you're saved". Mortality only in the Law half.
